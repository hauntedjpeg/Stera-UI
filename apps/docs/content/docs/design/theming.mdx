---
title: Theming
description: Rules to keep when you change Stera UI's color tokens.
---

Stera UI is a starting point, so you'll change its colors. The component design guidelines assume these rules still hold after you do. Whoever places a component can't affect them; whoever edits the tokens can.

## Contrast

- Button labels must meet WCAG AA contrast (4.5:1) against their background: `ink-onbrand` on `surface-brand` for `brand`, `ink-onaccent` on `surface-accent` for `accent`, and `ink` on `surface-subtle` for `subtle`.
- The `outline` border should be subtle, but distinct enough to meet accessibility contrast requirements against the page background.
- Badge labels must meet WCAG AA contrast (4.5:1) against their background in every tone and form: `ink-<tone>-bold` on `surface-<tone>-subtle` for the plain forms, `ink-on<tone>` on `surface-<tone>` for the `-solid` forms, and `ink-<tone>` on `surface` for the `-outline` forms. Watch `warning-outline`, whose label uses the lighter `ink-warning-subtle`.

## Distinct roles

- Keep `accent` visibly distinct from both `brand` and `danger`, so a strong button never leaves users unsure whether an action is the main one or a destructive one.
- Keep the `subtle` fill light enough that it doesn't compete with the primary action, and distinct enough that the button still reads as interactive.
- Keep the Badge tones `danger`, `success` and `warning` (`surface-danger`, `surface-success`, `surface-warning` and their `-subtle` fills) distinct from each other and from `brand` and `accent`, so a badge's tone reads as its meaning.
- Keep the Progress indicator (`surface-brand`) clearly distinct from its track (`surface-muted`), so how much is done reads at a glance.
- Keep the Toast type colors (`ink-success`, `ink-warning`, `ink-danger`, and `ink-subtler` for `info`) distinct from each other and from a toast's own `ink`, so a toast's type reads as its meaning.
- Keep the Skeleton fill (`surface-muted`) low in contrast against the surface behind it, so placeholders signal loading without drawing attention.

## Interactive states

- `subtle` and `ghost` buttons have little or no resting background, so their hover, focus and pressed states (`surface-subtle-hover`, `surface-hover`) carry most of the signal that they're interactive. Keep those states clear enough to tell the buttons apart from static text.
- A Toggle's pressed fill (`surface-subtle`) must be clearly distinct from its hover fill (`surface-hover`) and its focus ring (`ring`), so the active state reads at a glance. The same holds for Toggle Group items.
- Keep a Switch's checked track (`surface-brand`) clearly distinct from its unchecked track (`surface-muted`), so the state reads at a glance alongside the thumb's position.
- Keep the Slider's filled range (`surface-brand`) clearly distinct from the rest of its track (`surface-muted`), so the selected value or range reads at a glance.
- A Sidebar's active item (`surface-subtle`) must be clearly distinct from its hover fill (`surface-hover`), so the user's current location reads at a glance.
- Keep a Command's highlighted item (`surface-hover`) clearly distinct from the rest of the list, so keyboard users can track where they are.
- A Chip's active state (`border-border-bold`) must be clearly distinct from its hover fill (`surface-muted-hover` for `subtle`, `surface-hover` for `outline`) and its focus ring (`ring`). A `subtle` chip keeps its resting fill when active, so the border alone carries the selected state.
- A Table's selected row (`surface-subtle`) must be clearly distinct from its hover fill (`surface-hover`) and from unselected rows, so the selection stays visible as users scroll.
- A Calendar's selected date (`surface-brand`) must read unambiguously against unselected dates and against today (`surface-brand-subtle`). In a range, keep the in-range fill (`surface-muted`) distinct from both the start and end dates and the unselected dates, so the range reads as one continuous band.
